# Estate-rule sim: report (2026-11-05)

Question: does the unified estate rule (estate votes decide the estate's vote) dampen populism? Spec: `SPEC.md` (written first, four revisions, each before the results it affects). Numbers: `results.md` (235 cells, 40 replicates x 500 motions each, paired draws). Rerun: `python3 -I run.py` (about 11 minutes on 8 jobs; `--quick` for a smoke; `python3 -I test_estatesim.py` for the 52 tests). Output is byte-identical across reruns and across `--jobs` values.

## Answer

**Partly, weakly, and at a merit cost. The claim as stated ("dampens populism") is not established, and it fails in the cells that matter most.**

1. UNI lowers populism sensitivity (PS) against a direct tally in every sincere and strategic cell: by 0.02 to 0.07 on a DIRECT baseline of 0.23 to 0.35 (E1 equal weights: 0.297 to 0.275). Small, but outside the intervals.
2. It lowers merit tracking (MT) by more: 0.05 to 0.10 (E1: 0.465 to 0.400; with strategic agents and a 20-person Users faction: 0.381 to 0.304). Dampening populism by losing the signal is not what the claim means. PI = PS/MT, appeal sensitivity per unit of merit, is **worse** under UNI at equal weights (E1: 0.689 vs 0.639; E2 faction 20: 0.900 vs 0.849; E5 Users appeal .4: 0.840 vs 0.691) and flat under minds-heavy weights. The pre-registered verdict (SPEC section 8) therefore reads "supported" in 0 of 235 cells in the final run, "fails" in 2 (Users appeal loading b = .4, with and without delegation: MT drops more than .10), "undecided" in 233. Undecided here mostly means PS fell and MT fell too.
3. UNI passes more motions (0.65 vs 0.60 at equal weights) and passes more *bad popular* motions: BPP (pass rate when merit < -.5 and appeal > .5) is 0.62 vs 0.525 for DIRECT. It blocks fewer good unpopular ones (GBR 0.19 vs 0.23). The cause is the low-threshold categories: at q = .15 an estate casts all its weight For on 15% support, and PS at q = .15 collapses to 0.06 only because nearly everything passes. Per-category tables are in results.md. At q >= .4 UNI is within about .01 of DIRECT. The dampening is concentrated where the threshold was already doing the work.
4. **Winner-take-all does the opposite of dampening:** PS is above DIRECT by 0.03 to 0.06 in most cells (E1 equal: 0.323 vs 0.297; strategic with faction: 0.360 vs 0.323). Collapsing an estate to one bit at 0.5 amplifies a populist estate's swing; only the UNI threshold change (q instead of .5 inside the estate) makes the difference between the two bloc rules. If "unified estate rule" is read as WTA, the claim is **false** in this model.
5. A headcount tally (HEAD, reference only) is far worse (PS 0.43 to 0.52, MT collapses to 0.05 once the Users faction mobilizes). Estate structure of any kind does dampen against a mass-mobilized Users roll; the unified rule adds a little on top.

## Strategic agents

With 25% strategic voters (misreporting, abstaining, pivot-seeking turnout) strategy flips about 2.8% of outcomes under DIRECT, WTA and UNI alike and 1% under HEAD, and does not change the ranking of rules. A coordinated Users faction raises PS under DIRECT and WTA (0.291 at no faction to 0.328 at 40) while UNI stays flat at 0.27, so **the UNI-DIRECT gap widens with organized mobilization** (-0.024 to -0.054). This is the strongest point for the claim: UNI's resistance is to turnout-limited organized blocs, not to diffuse appeal. The MT gap also shrinks with faction size (-0.072 to -0.052), because the faction drags DIRECT's merit tracking down toward UNI's. Sweep E2.

## Delegation (E3, E3b, E3c, E3d, E3e, E4, E5b)

- **Delegation barely moves the claim.** dPS UNI-DIRECT is -0.027 with delegation vs -0.050 without in the matched strategic/faction cell; PS under each rule changes by under .01 (DIRECT .313 vs .323). Delegation makes the claim slightly weaker (the gap shrinks), not stronger.
- **Concentration is real and the cap controls it, but outcomes do not care.** Zipf exponent alpha from 0 to 4 moves the Users top-holder share from 0.045 to 0.19 (no cap) and n_eff from 61 to 26; a cap of .10 pulls that to 0.107, n_eff 44. A cap of .2 does not bind at alpha 1.5 (top share 0.103 with or without it) and binds only near alpha 4 (0.173 vs 0.190). The outcome measures (PS, MT) move by under .005 across the whole alpha x cap grid, because the Users estate's single vote is decided by its resolved majority and delegates are mostly drawn from the same population. Appeal attraction beta (E4) has the same null result. Cap basis (cast weight vs roll) matters only at small c (E3d): a roll-based cap never binds at 10% turnout.
- **Transitive depth 2, 3, 4, 6 and unlimited are indistinguishable** (E3c; PS and MT differ by under .005 across depths, including with 80% peer edges and delegates turning out at 30%). The votes that die do so on scope overlap, dead ends and absent peers (uncast share 0.50 to 0.85), not on length: a long chain is a chain whose scope intersection was already empty. Depth is a safe parameter to set at the placeholder 3 in this model; it does not need to be tuned. Loops are returned uncast and appear as part of the uncast share.
- **A delegation network under the posted revocation rule decays.** With two strikes and no renewal, 13% of delegations are alive after 500 motions (E3e), so the rule as posted cannot sustain liquid delegation unless revoked delegators re-delegate. The steady state depends on the renewal rate: 53% live at renew .02, 81% at .1. This is a design finding independent of the populism claim and was the cause of the first run's inert delegation cells.
- Delegation mostly leaves votes uncast (0.5 uncast with peer edges at the default). Whether delegators who lose a chain should be told is a product question the sim cannot answer.

## Copy-the-ballot (E3f, SPEC section 5b)

Source: Gölz et al. 2018 (introduction) argue, citing Behrens et al. 2014 (LiquidFeedback), that capping weight fails because a delegator can copy the delegate's ballot outside the system. It is an argument, not a measurement; the copy rate is an assumption and is swept (0, .5, 1). Agents: a delegator whose weight sits under an over-cap holder copies that holder's ballot with probability `copy` and votes directly with weight 1.

**Answer: once copying is available the cap does not reduce effective concentration.** Effective concentration counts a copier's weight to the voter it copied. Cap .10 against no cap, paired by replicate, 40 replicates (E3f top table in results.md):

- Without copying the cap works where concentration exists: effective Users top-holder share falls by 0.015 (alpha 1.5), 0.055 (2.5), 0.083 (4.0); below alpha 1.5 the cap does not bind.
- With full copying (copy = 1) the difference is +0.001 or less in every alpha, intervals about ±0.002: indistinguishable from no cap. At copy = .5 the difference is -0.008 at alpha 4 (under the one-point materiality bar), and under that at lower alpha. Verdict "reduces": 3 of 6 alphas at copy 0, 0 of 6 at .5, 0 of 6 at 1.
- The formal measure is misleading in the other direction: at copy = 1, alpha 4, cap .10 the formal top share drops from 0.190 to 0.034 (copiers appear as separate ballots), more than the cap achieves without copying (0.107). A cap would look best exactly when it is doing nothing to whose ballot the weight follows. It is also what the source's transparency point predicts: the system sees direct ballots and cannot see the copying.
- The cap's cost is paid anyway where copying is partial: copiers are 29% of delegated votes at alpha 4, cap .20, copy 1, and 57% at cap .10.
- Outcomes: dPS UNI-DIRECT stays -0.023 to -0.031 in all 108 cells, within intervals of the copy = 0 values. Copying changes nothing the outcome measures can see, as the cap alone did not (E3).
- Depth sweep (E3c) and every other experiment ran with copy = 0 and their results.md sections are byte-identical to the previous run.

Limits specific to this result: copying is triggered only when the holder is over the cap (a standing copy habit is not modeled); copiers copy the holder's sincere ballot and are not strategic; the cost of coordination grows with copiers in practice and is a flat probability here; the copy rate has no empirical value. At copy rates well below .5 the cap partly works; where the break-even lies was not mapped. The design question this leaves is not tuning c: a cap bounds the *ledger*, and a ledger-visible cap is evaded by a ballot copy that costs the delegator nothing but a few keystrokes. Whether the system wants delegation visible and capped, or invisible and uncapped, is a product choice for the operator; nothing is ruled here.

## Purchased stake (E6, SPEC section 5c)

Static weight analysis, not a motion simulation: it measures weight share, not PS or MT. Weight w = 1 + log_b(amount/unit), 0 below unit, per-estate b and unit (defaults b = 10, unit = 10 EUR, my choices, in SPEC 5c). The harness had no contribution mechanism, so contribution hours are a new per-agent input with their own per-estate conversion (defaults b = 10, unit = 10 hours); they add to the euro weight and matter to no buyer result below.

**The working hypothesis, that logarithmic weight bounds buyout power, is only half true here, and the true half is not the log's doing.**

- **Within the estate the log does not bound a million-euro buyer.** Against the harness's 4-patron roll (Pareto(1.16) donations from 10 EUR, 40 seeds) the buyer holds 0.524 +/- 0.008 of Patrons weight and alone decides the Patrons estate (share above one half) in 85% of seeds. The log shrinks the buyer from 100% (linear weight) to about half; it does not take it below a majority of a 4-person estate. At rolls of 40 and 400 the share falls (binding rates below).
- **At council level the bound comes from the estate weights.** With all four estates live the buyer's council share is 0.131 (equal weights) or 0.052 (minds-heavy), below the Patrons weight W_Patrons by construction; the half-influence cap never binds in 6,480 cell-seeds. Linear weight would give 0.25 and 0.10: the log halves the buyer's share, the estate structure sets its ceiling.
- **The half-influence cap binds only when other estates are silent.** Fraction of (cell, seed) in which council share x >= .5 over 162 cells (b x unit x roll x amount x weights) x 40 seeds: all four live 0.000; Patrons + Minds live 0.044 (all of it the degenerate case where no other patron clears the unit, so there is nothing to cut against: unenforceable); Patrons alone live 0.222 (0.148 of cells bind in every seed, 0.089 of cell-seeds unenforceable). It binds more at small rolls (Patrons alone: 0.669 at roll 4 and b = 2 down to 0.000 at roll 400) and with a smaller log base. The Patrons-alone scenario is the renormalization case of prior section 10 (drop non-voting estates); how likely it is in practice is not a sim output.
- **Splitting wins, by orders of magnitude.** The same 1,000,000 EUR over k identities multiplies weight by 1.9 (k = 2), 8.3 (k = 10), 66.7 (k = 100), 500 (k = 1000), and 16,667 at k = amount/unit = 100,000 (each fake identity buying exactly the unit). The multiplier is (amount/unit) / (1 + log_b(amount/unit)); for b = 10, unit = 10 it is 250 at 10,000 EUR, 2,000 at 100,000 EUR. With k = 10 fake identities the buyer's Patrons-alone share is 0.901, with k = 2 it is 0.676. This refutes the log as a purchase bound unless identities are scarce.
- **The half-influence cap is per identity and splitting evades it.** In the Patrons-alone case the single identity trips the cap in 85% of seeds; at k = 2 no identity does (each holds 0.34) while the joint share is 0.676, in every seed. A cap on a person's influence needs an identity oracle the harness does not have.
- **SPEC names no sybil defence** (the charter's frozen roll, A6, bounds joins after notice, not pre-notice buying of identities), so none is implemented as policy. Three candidates are quantified in results.md and none is chosen: a per-person weight cap does not help (it caps the honest single purchase and the fakes are each below it; 100,000x at cap 1); a raised unit floor reduces the multiplier (250 at 1,000 EUR, 5 at 100,000 EUR, 1.0 only at 1,000,000 EUR, where no purchase under the buyer's amount buys any weight), so it neutralises splitting only by pricing out everyone smaller; a per-identity cost neutralises only at about half or more of the budget (51% to 82% on the doubling grid across 10,000 to 1,000,000 EUR), so it would also tax the honest buyer's single identity at that rate. The choice between pricing identities, verifying people or bounding total weight per verified person is a design fork left for the operator. Nothing here rules it.

Limits specific to E6: the roll, the Pareto scale, b and unit are assumptions; the buyer joins as an additional patron and buys once (no repeated purchase or liquidation); decisiveness is estate-level only, not council-level (prior section 10 shows weight share is not decisiveness); the best split is a grid maximum, a lower bound; hours play no part in a purchase scenario because estate weights are fixed and the buyer sits in Patrons, so an hours buyer (a Member who is paid or who farms hours) is untested and the conversion of hours into weight is just a per-estate parameter whose gaming is not modeled.

## Robustness (E5, E5b)

The sign of the PS effect holds across Users appeal 0.4/0.9/1.4, Users turnout .05/.10/.30 and Minds shared taste .3/.9/1.5. Its size scales with Users appeal loading (-0.025 at .4, -0.067 at 1.4): the rule helps in proportion to how appeal-driven the Users estate is, while its merit loss does not go away (MT gap -0.101 at .4 vs -0.063 at 1.4). If Users are not appeal-driven the rule is nearly all cost.

## Limits and honesty

- Every behavioral parameter is an assumption (SPEC section 4), not data. The populism measure rests on an independent merit/appeal draw that the real system cannot observe; PS is an error rate against a modeled channel, not a measurement of any real vote.
- Myopic, single-motion strategy; no repeated game, vote trading, or reputation. Delegators are not strategic. Prior work (../2026-10-29-estate-tally-game-theory/) covers some of those on unitary blocs.
- No Patron/Member inequality inside estates; no real Bifrost data.
- The verdict rule counts MT loss over .05 as "not supported" and over .10 as "fails". These are my pre-registered thresholds; a different pair would change the verdict column but not the PS/MT numbers beside it.
- Sources: SPEC section 10 ([V] fetched, [M] from memory). The delegate-cap "a fifth" has no strong precedent found; DAO caps are rare and the Jupiter 5% proposal is the nearest.

## Deviations from the spec text

- E6: the brief said the half-influence cap and an 'E3f cap sweep' were already in the harness. The harness has only the delegation cap of SPEC section 5 (a fraction of cast weight per final holder); the operator cap of prior section 10 (council weight share strictly below one half) was not implemented, so `influence_cap` implements it, once, in `estatesim.py`; the E3f sweep is of the delegation cap and is untouched. E6 is a static weight analysis, not integrated into the motion simulation (no PS or MT for buyers).
- Spec rev 3 added renewal (`renew`) and peer edges (`peer`) after the first full run showed (a) revocation had silently emptied every delegation cell at 500 motions and (b) with only delegates delegating onward, depth was inert. The first run's results were discarded. Renewal and peer defaults are my choices, swept in E3c and E3e.
- Cap basis default is the estate's cast weight, not roll; both are swept.
- E3f: the falsifier was first printed as means only; the paired interval was added to `run.py` before the final run (no number changed). Its materiality bar (-.01) is mine.
- Per-category PS tables are printed only for the non-grid experiments (E1, E2, E4, E5).
